    Top 10 Gifts for Gamers (PC, Console and VR Picks They'll Use)

    Tech · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

    Shopping for a gamer can feel like walking into a conversation halfway through. They talk about frame rates, switches and party chat, and their setup already seems to have everything. The good news is that gamers almost always have a short list of upgrades in the back of their mind: a better headset, a second controller for co-op, a mouse that isn't held together with tape.

    This list is part of our top 10 series for hobbies and personalities. It mixes PC, Xbox, Switch and VR gifts, so you can match the pick to the platform they actually play on. Most picks come straight from our Best Gifts for Gamers guide, with a few favorites pulled from our tech guides.

    How we chose these gamer gifts

    We stuck to well-known gaming brands like Logitech, Xbox, Nintendo, HyperX, Razer, SteelSeries and Elgato, and we based each description on what the product is designed to do according to its listing. We covered every budget, from desk upgrades to a full VR headset, and included honest downsides for each one.

    1. Logitech G305 LIGHTSPEED Wireless Gaming Mouse

    The Logitech G305 LIGHTSPEED is a lightweight wireless gaming mouse with lag-free LIGHTSPEED connectivity and long battery life. It's one of the easiest upgrades for any PC gamer still using a basic office mouse, and it's small enough to be a generous stocking stuffer.

    Downsides: It runs on a battery rather than a built-in rechargeable cell, so keep spares around. Gamers with very large hands may prefer a bigger shape.

    2. Xbox Wireless Controller

    The official Xbox Wireless Controller has textured grips and Bluetooth, so it works on Xbox, PC and mobile. A second controller is the gift that makes couch co-op possible, and it's handy for PC gamers who prefer a pad for racing and action games.

    Downsides: It's a solid all-rounder, not a pro controller with extra paddles. If they already own two, look elsewhere on this list.

    3. HyperX Cloud III Wired Gaming Headset

    The HyperX Cloud III is a comfy, clear-sounding headset with a good mic for chatting with friends mid-game. It's a top choice across PC and consoles, which makes it a safe bet if you aren't sure what they play on.

    Downsides: It's wired, so they'll be tethered to the controller or PC. Gamers who move around a lot may want a wireless model instead.

    Author's take

    The author uses the HyperX Cloud III as his everyday gaming headset. The sound is great for the price, and the spatial surround sound is a real help in FPS games. He pairs it with a separate HyperX QuadCast S microphone with adjustable RGB lighting, and people he plays with say they can hear him very clearly.

    4. Razer BlackShark V2 X Gaming Headset

    The Razer BlackShark V2 X is a light, comfortable headset with 50mm drivers, 7.1 surround sound and memory foam cushions. It plugs into PC, PlayStation and Switch with a standard 3.5mm jack, so it's an easy pick for a teen or a gamer on a budget. You'll also find it in our Black Friday tech under $100 guide.

    Downsides: Like the HyperX, it's wired. Pick one headset, not both, unless they need a spare for a second console.

    5. Nintendo Switch Pro Controller

    The Nintendo Switch Pro Controller is Nintendo's premium controller with motion controls, rumble and long battery life. It makes long Switch sessions far more comfortable than the small Joy-Con controllers, especially for adult hands.

    Downsides: It's a pricier controller, and it's built for Nintendo's consoles. If they only play handheld on the go, it may sit on the shelf.

    6. Keychron K2 Wireless Mechanical Keyboard

    A mechanical keyboard is a satisfying upgrade for PC gamers who also type a lot. The Keychron K2 is a compact 75% wireless mechanical keyboard that works with Mac and Windows. You can find it on Amazon or directly from Keychron's site, and it also appears in our desk and office gifts guide.

    Downsides: Mechanical keyboards are louder than laptop keys, and keyboard fans have strong opinions about switch types. Check the switch option on the listing before you order.

    7. SteelSeries QcK XXL Gaming Mouse Pad

    The SteelSeries QcK XXL is an extra-large desk mat that fits a keyboard and mouse with room to spare. It instantly makes a gaming setup look cleaner and gives low-sensitivity players space for big mouse sweeps.

    Downsides: Measure their desk first. An XXL pad on a small desk will hang off the edge.

    8. Govee RGBIC TV LED Backlight

    The Govee RGBIC TV LED Backlight adds color-changing lights behind the screen that sync with what's on it, for more immersive gaming and movie nights. It's a fun setup upgrade for console players who game on a TV.

    Downsides: This version is sized for 55 to 65 inch TVs, so check their screen size. It also takes a little patience to stick on and set up.

    Author's take

    The author runs several Govee lights from his Stream Deck.

    9. Elgato Stream Deck MK.2

    The Elgato Stream Deck MK.2 puts 15 customizable LCD keys on the desk to launch apps, shortcuts and scenes in one tap. It's a favorite for gamers who stream or record clips, and it's handy for switching audio, muting a mic or opening Discord. If they're creating content too, see our gifts for photographers and creators guide.

    Downsides: It shines with a PC setup and a bit of time spent customizing. Console-only gamers won't get much out of it.

    Author's take

    The author uses his Stream Deck+, the version with dials, every day to launch games, adjust volumes, and switch his Govee lights: wave lights, monitor backlights, light bars and floor lamps. One button press and the whole setup changes. The MK.2 in this list gives you the same one-press shortcuts without the dials.

    10. Meta Quest 3S VR Headset

    The Meta Quest 3S is an affordable, all-in-one VR headset for gaming, fitness and immersive entertainment. Because it's standalone, there's no gaming PC required, which makes it a true wow-factor gift for gamers of all ages.

    Downsides: It's the biggest spend on this list, and VR needs clear floor space. Some people also feel motion sickness in VR, so it's worth asking if they've tried it before.

    Quick picks by budget and platform

    • Stocking stuffers and small gifts: Logitech G305 mouse or SteelSeries QcK XXL mouse pad
    • Mid-range: Xbox controller, HyperX Cloud III, Razer BlackShark V2 X or Govee TV backlight
    • Bigger gifts: Switch Pro Controller, Keychron K2 or Stream Deck MK.2
    • Splurge: Meta Quest 3S

    Tips for buying gaming gifts

    • Confirm the platform. Xbox, PlayStation, Switch and PC accessories aren't always interchangeable.
    • Upgrade the thing they use every day. Headsets, mice and controllers wear out, and a better one gets used for years.
    • Watch the sales. Gaming gear often drops around Black Friday, so keep an eye on our Black Friday tech under $100 picks.

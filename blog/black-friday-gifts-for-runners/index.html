    Black Friday Gifts for Runners: 3 Picks Worth Watching

    Tech · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

    Runners are hard to shop for, and the gear they'd really like, such as a proper GPS watch, is what they talk themselves out of buying. Black Friday is a good time to close that gap. Here are three running gifts that Amazon is marking down right now, what each one actually does, and who should skip it.

    Amazon's early Black Friday deals are already live, and Amazon says its Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday event runs November 19 through November 30. When we checked on the evening of November 12, all three listings below showed a markdown from list price. For a wider set of workout and recovery ideas, see Penny's home gym and fitness gifts guide.

    Quick picks:

    • Best for tracking runs and training: Garmin Forerunner 165 running watch
    • Best for running with music while hearing traffic: SHOKZ OpenRun Pro open-ear headphones
    • Best for early-morning and after-dark runs: Black Diamond Spot 400-R rechargeable headlamp

    How we chose these picks

    We looked at running watches, sport headphones and running safety gear on Amazon, then kept only items whose listing showed a markdown from list price on November 12. That cut a few popular options. The Garmin Forerunner 70, Apple Watch SE 3, SHOKZ OpenRun Pro 2 and Theragun Mini didn't show a markdown in their main price box when we checked, even though some deal sites have listed them.

    Every feature below comes from the Amazon listings and Amazon's summaries of customer reviews. We haven't tested these ourselves, and we leave prices out because they change.

    1. Garmin Forerunner 165 (Best for tracking runs and training)

    The Garmin Forerunner 165 Running Smartwatch is Garmin's easy-to-use running watch. It has built-in GPS for pace and distance, wrist-based heart rate, and an AMOLED touchscreen that still keeps physical buttons, which helps with sweaty fingers or gloves. Garmin lists up to 11 days of battery in smartwatch mode and up to 19 hours with GPS on.

    What makes it different: It doesn't just record runs. It gives daily suggested workouts that adapt to recent performance and recovery, and Garmin Coach and race training plans can build a plan around a specific event. Each morning it shows a report with sleep, recovery, HRV status and the day's training outlook, and after hard sessions it shows how long to recover. Incident detection can send a live location to emergency contacts during select outdoor activities when paired with a phone. It has a 4.3-star average from more than 5,000 ratings, and Amazon shows more than 6,000 bought in the past month.

    Downsides: Amazon's review summary says many buyers see four to six days between charges in real use, which is well short of the 11-day figure. Some buyers also report phone connection problems, and one review says it's better for running than for counting gym sets. Garmin also sells the newer Forerunner 70, but the markdown we found was on the 165. The black version had the deepest markdown when we checked.

    Who it's for: Someone training for a 5K, half marathon or their first marathon who wants real guidance, not just a step count. Skip it if they already run with a recent Garmin or Apple Watch, or if they want a watch mainly for apps and notifications.

    2. SHOKZ OpenRun Pro (Best for running with music while hearing traffic)

    The SHOKZ OpenRun Pro Bone Conduction Headphones sit in front of the ears instead of in them and pass sound through the cheekbones. That leaves the ears open, so the runner can still hear cars, bikes and other people on the path. A wraparound titanium frame holds them in place, and they're built to resist sweat.

    What makes it different: Most earbuds block outside sound or need a special mode to let it in. These are open by design, which is the main reason runners who use roads pick them. SHOKZ lists 10 hours of music and calls, and a 5-minute quick charge adds up to 1.5 hours. They have a 4.3-star average from more than 22,000 ratings, so a lot of people have used them.

    Downsides: Bone conduction doesn't sound like good earbuds, and Amazon's review summary shows real disagreement on sound quality. Reliability and durability also get mixed feedback, with some buyers saying the microphone stopped working after a few months. Opinions on value are split too. SHOKZ sells a newer OpenRun Pro 2 that wasn't marked down when we checked.

    Who it's for: Road runners and anyone who runs in traffic, on shared paths or with a group and wants to stay aware. Skip it if they mainly run on a treadmill and care most about rich sound, since sealed earbuds will sound better.

    3. Black Diamond Spot 400-R Headlamp (Best for early-morning and after-dark runs)

    The Black Diamond Spot 400-R Rechargeable Headlamp puts out up to 400 lumens and lists night running among its uses. It runs on a built-in 1500mAh battery that charges over micro-USB, so there are no disposable batteries to buy. It's rated IP67, which the listing says means it can survive full submersion for up to 30 minutes. Modes include full power, dimming, strobe, red night vision and a lock mode so it doesn't turn on in a bag.

    What makes it different: It had the biggest markdown of the three when we checked. It's also the most practical gift in winter, when many runners start and finish in the dark. Amazon's review summary says buyers like its brightness, simple controls and low weight.

    Downsides: It charges with micro-USB, not USB-C, which means one more old cable. The same review summary says battery life and durability are mixed, with some buyers saying it stopped charging or only lasted about an hour and a half. A headlamp helps them see, but they'll still want reflective gear so drivers can see them.

    Who it's for: Anyone who runs before sunrise or after work in the winter, especially on trails or unlit roads. Skip it if they only run on a treadmill or on well-lit city streets.

    Which one should you pick?

    For a runner who's working toward a race, the Garmin Forerunner 165 gives the most day-to-day value. For someone who runs on roads and already has a watch, the SHOKZ OpenRun Pro solves the safety problem with earbuds. If you want something useful that's hard to get wrong, the Black Diamond headlamp is the safest choice, and it had the deepest markdown. Include a gift receipt with the watch and headphones, since fit and feel are personal.

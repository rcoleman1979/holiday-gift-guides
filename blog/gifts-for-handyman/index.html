    Top 10 Gifts for a Handyman (Tools They'll Actually Use)

    For Him · October 27, 2026 · 5 min read

    Every family has one. The person who shows up with a toolbox when a door sticks, who patches the drywall after a move, who knows which breaker is which without looking. Shopping for a handyman can feel tricky, because they already own a lot of tools and they're picky about the ones they use.

    The trick is to skip the novelty gadgets and give them gear that saves time on the jobs they do every week. This list is part of our top 10 series for hobbies and personalities, and every pick comes from our Top 10 Cool Tools guide. It works just as well for a DIY dad, a new homeowner or the friend who is always fixing everyone else's stuff.

    How we chose these handyman gifts

    We picked tools from trusted names like DEWALT, Milwaukee, Klein, Leatherman and Bosch, and we leaned on what each product is designed to do according to its listing. We covered a wide range of budgets, from stocking stuffers to a serious power tool, and we noted honest downsides so you can choose with your eyes open. We left out anything that depends on a battery platform they might not own unless it comes as a full kit.

    1. DEWALT 20V MAX XR Drill/Driver Kit

    The DEWALT 20V MAX XR Drill/Driver Kit is the headline gift on this list. A cordless drill/driver is the tool a handyman reaches for on almost every job, from hanging shelves to assembling furniture to building a deck. Because it's a kit, it's ready to go out of the box.

    Downsides: If they're already invested in another brand's batteries, a second platform means extra chargers and packs. Ask around quietly about what's in their garage first.

    2. LEATHERMAN Surge 21-in-1 Multi-Tool

    The LEATHERMAN Surge packs 21 tools into one heavy-duty multi-tool, so the handyman always has pliers, blades and drivers on their belt. It's the kind of gift that gets carried every day for years.

    Downsides: It's a full-size multi-tool, so it's heavier than a pocket knife. If they want something slim for everyday carry, this may feel like too much.

    3. Milwaukee 9-in-1 Ratcheting Driver

    The Milwaukee 9-in-1 Ratcheting Driver swaps between bits and nut drivers in one handle, and the ratchet means less wrist twisting on long screws. It is a smart pick under $50 that a pro will still appreciate.

    Downsides: Multi-bit drivers can lose bits if they're tossed loose in a bag. A magnetic tray or the wristband below helps.

    4. Klein Tools 11-in-1 Screwdriver

    Klein is a name electricians trust, and the Klein Tools 11-in-1 Screwdriver is a classic for a reason. It covers common screw and nut sizes in one tool, which makes it an easy stocking stuffer and a great glovebox or kitchen-drawer backup.

    Downsides: Plenty of handymen already own one. That's not a bad thing, since a spare for the truck or the house rarely goes unused.

    5. Bosch Blaze Pro 165 ft Laser Measure

    The Bosch Blaze Pro laser measure reads distances up to 165 feet at the press of a button, which is a lifesaver for measuring rooms, ordering flooring or planning a paint job without a second person holding the tape.

    Downsides: A laser measure won't replace a tape measure for cuts and small work, and it needs a surface to bounce off. Think of it as a partner to the tape, not a swap.

    6. Magnetic Wristband for Screws & Nails

    Simple, cheap and surprisingly useful, the magnetic wristband holds screws, nails and small bits right on their wrist while they work on a ladder or under a sink. It's one of the best stocking stuffers for any DIYer.

    Downsides: Magnets only hold steel hardware, so it won't help with brass or stainless screws.

    7. Fanttik S1 Pro Electric Screwdriver

    The Fanttik S1 Pro Electric Screwdriver is a compact rechargeable driver for small jobs like electronics, battery covers, furniture and light fixtures, where a full drill is overkill. (#ad) If they love clever gadgets like this, our cleaning gadget gifts guide has more of Fanttik's lineup.

    Downsides: It's built for light-duty work, so it's not the tool for driving long deck screws. Pair it with a real drill rather than replacing one.

    8. Franklin ProSensor M210 Stud Finder

    Anyone who has drilled into drywall and missed the stud knows why the Franklin ProSensor M210 Stud Finder makes a great gift. It's designed to show where studs are across its whole face, which makes hanging TVs, shelves and cabinets less of a guessing game.

    Downsides: Like any stud finder, results can vary with thick plaster, tile or unusual wall materials. Double-check before drilling into anything that matters.

    9. Black Diamond Spot 400-R Headlamp

    Handymen spend a lot of time in dark places: under sinks, in attics and crawl spaces, and behind appliances. The Black Diamond Spot 400-R is a rechargeable headlamp that keeps both hands free. It also doubles as camping gear, so it's a good fit for anyone in our outdoor and camping gifts guide.

    Downsides: Rechargeable means remembering to charge it. Tuck a USB charger near their workbench.

    10. GEARWRENCH 20-Piece Ratcheting Wrench Set

    The GEARWRENCH 20-piece ratcheting wrench set is a big upgrade over standard combination wrenches, because the ratcheting end keeps turning without lifting off the bolt. It's a favorite for car work and tight spaces, so it also belongs on our gifts for car lovers list.

    Downsides: It's a larger set and a bigger spend than a basic wrench kit. Check whether they work mostly in metric or SAE sizes before you buy.

    Quick picks by budget

    • Stocking stuffers: magnetic wristband or Klein 11-in-1 screwdriver
    • Under $50: Milwaukee 9-in-1 ratcheting driver or Fanttik S1 Pro
    • Under $100: Bosch Blaze laser measure, Franklin stud finder or Black Diamond headlamp
    • Splurge: DEWALT drill/driver kit, LEATHERMAN Surge or GEARWRENCH wrench set

    Tips for buying tools as a gift

    • Peek at their brand. Many handymen are loyal to one battery platform, so power tools should match.
    • Upgrade, don't duplicate. If they already have a basic version of something, a better or more specialized one is usually welcome.
    • Include a gift receipt. Tool people have strong opinions, and that's okay.
    • Shop the sales. Tools often get discounted around Black Friday, so check our Black Friday deals worth watching guide.

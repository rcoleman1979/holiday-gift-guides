    Black Friday Gifts for PC Builders: 4 Picks Worth Watching

    Tech · November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

    Buying PC parts for someone else is risky. A processor that doesn't fit their motherboard is a paperweight, a drive with no free slot is a return, and a monitor their graphics card can't drive is wasted money. The safest PC builder gifts either drop into a standard platform or improve the desk around the tower. Here are four picks Amazon has on early Black Friday promotion, what each actually does, and who should skip it.

    Amazon started its early Black Friday deals on November 10, ahead of its official Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday event (November 19 through November 30). That same morning, the deal tracker BottleneckPC flagged all four of these items on Amazon as either below their usual Amazon price or carrying an Amazon limited-time deal. Deals change fast, so check each listing for what's live today. For a wider list of setup upgrades, see Penny's gaming and streaming setup upgrades guide.

    Quick picks:

    • Best upgrade for a gaming build: AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D
    • Best for a crowded game library: Sandisk Optimus GX 7100 4TB SSD
    • Best splurge for the desk: ASUS ROG Strix XG27ACDNG QD-OLED monitor
    • Best for builders who also work at their PC: Logitech MX Master 3S (Bluetooth Edition)

    How we chose these picks

    We started with PC parts and peripherals that deal trackers listed as discounted on Amazon on November 10, then kept one pick per job so nothing overlaps: processor, storage, display and mouse. We checked every Amazon listing that evening, and each feature below comes from them. We haven't tested these products ourselves, and we leave prices out because they change.

    1. AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D (Best upgrade for a gaming build)

    The AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D is the gift for a builder who mostly games. Amazon's listing describes 8 cores and 16 threads on AMD's Zen 5 architecture, 96MB of L3 cache using AMD's next-gen 3D V-Cache, and boost clocks up to 5.2GHz. AMD markets it as a gaming processor, and that extra cache is the reason. It's drop-in ready for Socket AM5.

    What makes it different: Compared with a regular eight-core chip, the X3D's large stacked cache is the selling point for games. Deal tracker ByteSizedDeals also marked Amazon's November 10 listing as a promo price.

    Downsides: The cooler isn't included, so they'll need one. It only fits AM5 motherboards, so it's not an upgrade for an older AM4 or Intel system without a new board and DDR5 memory. If they mainly edit video or run heavy workloads, a chip with more cores may suit them better.

    Who it's for: Gamers already on AM5, or someone planning a new AM5 build this winter. Skip it unless you know their socket, or if they'd rather pick the part themselves.

    2. Sandisk Optimus GX 7100 4TB SSD (Best for a crowded game library)

    The Sandisk Optimus GX 7100 4TB is the lower-risk part gift. Most modern builds have an M.2 slot. Amazon lists it as a PCIe Gen 4.0 NVMe drive in the common M.2 2280 size, built on Sandisk's 8th-generation BiCS TLC NAND with nCache technology. It comes with the Sandisk Dashboard app (Windows only) for updates and a Game Mode, plus Acronis True Image for Sandisk to move an existing system over.

    What makes it different: Size is the reason to pick this drive. At 4TB, it can hold a big library without anyone having to delete games to make room. The listing's headline speeds (up to 7,250MB/s read and 6,900MB/s write) are quoted for the 1TB and 2TB models, so don't assume the 4TB hits the same numbers.

    Downsides: It's PCIe 4.0, not the newer 5.0, and the listing doesn't say anything about a heatsink. Before you buy, make sure they have a free M.2 slot.

    Who it's for: Gamers whose drive is full, and new builds that need a roomy main drive. Skip it if their PC has no free M.2 slot, or if they specifically want a PCIe 5.0 drive.

    3. ASUS ROG Strix XG27ACDNG QD-OLED Monitor (Best splurge for the desk)

    The ASUS ROG Strix XG27ACDNG is the big gift on this list, and the one they'll look at every day. Amazon lists a 26.5-inch QHD (2560 x 1440) QD-OLED panel with a 360Hz refresh rate, a 0.03ms response time and G-SYNC Compatible support. For color and HDR, it lists VESA DisplayHDR 400 True Black, 99% DCI-P3 coverage and true 10-bit color. BottleneckPC listed its Amazon limited-time deal as running through November 30.

    What makes it different: OLED burn-in is the usual worry, and ASUS addresses it head-on. The listing describes a custom heatsink, OLED Care+ features to reduce the risk of burn-in, and a 3-year warranty that covers burn-in.

    Downsides: It's 1440p, not 4K. Reaching 360 frames per second takes a strong graphics card in most games, so a modest PC won't use all of that refresh rate.

    Who it's for: Competitive and fast-paced gamers with a capable GPU, and anyone ready to upgrade from an older LCD. Skip it if they want a 4K screen for work, or if their graphics card is entry level.

    4. Logitech MX Master 3S Bluetooth Edition (Best for builders who also work at their PC)

    The Logitech MX Master 3S Bluetooth Edition is for the builder whose PC pays the bills, too. Amazon lists an 8,000 DPI sensor that tracks on almost any surface (including glass), Quiet Clicks, MagSpeed scrolling, an ergonomic shape with thumb controls, app-specific button profiles in Logi Options+, and Logitech Flow for moving the cursor, text and files between Windows and macOS computers.

    What makes it different: This is a productivity mouse, not a gaming mouse. The fast scroll wheel and cross-computer control matter more here than raw speed, which makes it a good match for someone who games on one setup and works on a laptop next to it.

    Downsides: This edition is Bluetooth only, and Amazon says it doesn't include a USB receiver or charging cable. Many desktop motherboards without Wi-Fi also lack Bluetooth, so check their PC first. It's a right-handed shape.

    Who it's for: Builders who code, edit or work from home on the same desk. Skip it if they want a lightweight gaming mouse, or if their tower has no Bluetooth.

    Which one should you pick?

    Unless you know their exact platform, start with the gifts that don't depend on it. The monitor and mouse work with almost any PC, and the SSD only needs a free M.2 slot.
